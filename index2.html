Memoryless property

The exponential distribution is memoryless. This means that if no request has arrived during the first t₁ seconds, the probability of waiting at least another (t₂ − t₁) seconds is the same as the probability of waiting that long from the start.

P(T > t₂ | T > t₁) = P(T > t₂ − t₁) = e−λ(t₂−t₁)

Central Limit Theorem — Warehouse Processing Time

Calculate the probability of average warehouse order-processing time using the Central Limit Theorem and the sampling distribution of the sample mean.

Warehouse application: A warehouse processes customer orders. Individual processing times have a population mean μ and standard deviation σ. A random sample of n orders is selected. The Central Limit Theorem helps estimate the probability that the sample average processing time falls within a specified range.

Warehouse input parameters

Enter the population statistics, sample size and required probability condition.

For sufficiently large random samples, the Central Limit Theorem allows the sample mean to be approximated by a normal distribution. For small samples, the population distribution should be normal or the approximation should be used cautiously.

Understanding the warehouse application

The Central Limit Theorem helps warehouse managers estimate the probability of average order-processing times for samples of orders. Even when individual processing times are not normally distributed, the sampling distribution of the sample mean becomes approximately normal under suitable conditions as the sample size increases.

Population mean = μ
Population standard deviation = σ
Sample mean = X̄
Standard error = σ / √n
Sampling variance = σ² / n
