Welcome to your probability lab ✧

Choose a distribution to calculate probabilities and visualize its behaviour.

◈ Binomial

Counts the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials.

P(X=k) = C(n,k)pᵏ(1-p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ

Example: correct predictions out of 20.

✧ Poisson

Counts events occurring within a fixed interval.

P(X=k) = e⁻λ λᵏ / k!

Example: security alerts per hour.

✦ Geometric

Counts the trials needed to obtain the first success.

P(X=k) = (1-p)ᵏ⁻¹p

Example: attempts until a solution is found.

Experiment 1 — Binomial Quality Control

For n = 20 inspected products and defect probability p = 0.05, calculate the mean, variance and P(X = 2). View the PMF graph, then change p to 0.10 and observe how probability shifts toward larger defect counts.

Mean = np · Variance = np(1 − p) · P(X = 2) = C(20,2)p²(1 − p)¹⁸

Experiment 2 — Binomial Alert Monitoring

Set n = 100 and p = 0.0225. Calculate the probability of exactly 3 failures and inspect the PMF graph.

P(X = 3) = C(100,3)(0.0225)³(1 − 0.0225)⁹⁷

Experiment 3 — Poisson Defect Counts

For 100 inspected products, set λ = 5 and compare the Poisson probabilities with the Binomial model. Review P(X = 8) and the cumulative probability P(X ≤ 5).

P(X = k) = e⁻⁵ 5ᵏ / k! · Mean = Variance = λ = 5

Experiment 4 — Poisson Alert Monitoring

Set λ = 3 and examine the probabilities of 0, 1, 2, 3 and higher alert counts. Then compare λ = 2, λ = 3 and λ = 5 to see how the distribution shifts as the average alert count increases.

P(X = k) = e⁻λ λᵏ / k! · P(X > 3) = 1 − P(X ≤ 3)

Experiment 5 — Geometric Distribution

For p = 0.05, examine the probability that the first defect is detected on different inspection attempts. The alert-monitoring experiment also asks you to use p = 0.8 and calculate success on the first, second and third attempts.

P(X = k) = (1 − p)ᵏ⁻¹p · P(X = 1) = p

Experiment 6 — Interpret the Results

Record the numerical results and graph observations. Discuss whether the probabilities can help an operations team set monitoring thresholds, investigate failures, and evaluate retry policies.

Observe the Binomial PMF peak and its shift when p changes; compare Poisson curves as λ increases; and note that for the Geometric distribution, the probability of the first success on attempt k decreases across attempts when p is fixed and between 0 and 1.
